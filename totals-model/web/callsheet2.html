Enter both prices on every market you want ranked. One price says the lean; both say the hold, and the hold is what decides how much of the lean is believed. A market with no prices still gets a probability — it just cannot be ranked, because there is nothing to rank it against.

The moneyline is the split. The book's total says how many runs; the book's moneyline says whose. Together they pin down each side's expected runs, and every other market here is arithmetic on that pair. Without a moneyline there is no run line and no moneyline call.

This matchup, ranked by chance to hit

Ordered by how likely each pick is to hit, because that is what compounds in a parlay and what an odds boost is spent on. The gold or red figure on every row is the edge: how far above or below the price's break-even that chance is. A 64% favourite at −180 is a 64% chance and, at that price, a losing bet — the boost is what makes it worth taking. Tick rank by edge to see the board the way a straight-bet player would.

Why

    Day board

    The parlay four likeliest leg per game, within your price cap
    Best straight bets by edge — the price beats the chance

    The parlay four takes the likeliest priced market from each game, one leg per game, and keeps the four likeliest. A leg priced worse than the leg cap (−170 by default) is swapped for the next-likeliest market on the same game, and the card says what it replaced — a −275 favourite is a fine result and a poor parlay leg. All four hit is the product of the four chances: compare it to what the boosted parlay pays, and a payout better than that multiple is a parlay worth having.

    Best straight bets is the other ranking — the edge, where the chance beats what the price needs — and only rows with a positive edge qualify. Both fours are graded separately in the record. Click any pick card or table row to load that matchup into the form.

    The four at the top are the four to look at, not the four to bet. Two markets on the same game are one bet in disguise — a run line and an under both cash on a pitchers' duel — so a row that shares a game with a higher one is flagged corr. Re-read the inputs on anything that ranks first: in this project a spectacular number has more often been a typo than an edge.
