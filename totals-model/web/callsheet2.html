The moneyline is the split. The book's total says how many runs; the book's moneyline says whose. Together they pin down each side's expected runs, and every other market here is arithmetic on that pair. Without a moneyline there is no run line and no moneyline call.

This matchup, ranked by edge

Probability is the big number; edge is the order. Each row shows how likely the pick is to hit and, in gold or red, how far above or below the price's break-even that is. A 62% favourite at −185 is a 62% chance and a losing bet; a 53% under at −110 is the better bet on the same game. The green mark is this game's parlay leg — Call Sheet #1's BET on the total when that side clears its price inside your leg cap, otherwise the side with the best chance against its price — and an amber mark names the likeliest thing on the game when it is priced beyond the cap. Two grey chips on the total are labels, not adjustments: cold-under profile (total 7 or lower, wind in at 10 mph or more, both pens under 3.75, a last ten below the line) and same lean or split lean (whether the full-game pick and the first-five pick agree). An amber over against the grain chip marks an over that the book's prices, the last ten, or 80%+ of the money leans against — overs like that are 11-15 on the season book against 79-44 for the rest. None of these moves a number; each has its own line in the record below, so it can earn one.

    The parlay four #1's BET on the total first, else the best value side — one per game, inside your cap, only if it clears its price
    Best straight bets by edge — the price beats the chance
    One sport only the same two rules, MLB and WNBA apart — for a night you want just one

    The parlay four takes one leg per game, in two tiers. First, Call Sheet #1's verdict: a full-game total #1 calls BET, STRONG BET or MAX BET, on #1's side, when that side also clears its price inside your leg cap (−170 by default) — the one mark on the board that carries #1's corroboration gate. Second, on a game with no such total: the side, of any market priced inside the cap, with the best chance against what its price needs. In both tiers only a side that clears its price qualifies — a parlay's return is the product of those ratios, a boost multiplies the whole thing, and a leg priced above its chance drags it down however often it hits, so a 55% over at −120 beats a 61% dog at −155. Verdict legs rank first, then by value; the card names what each leg passed on. On a thin night you get three, or two, and the card says why. All four hit is the product of the chances — a boosted payout above that multiple beats fair outright — and worth N× is the product of the ratios, the parlay's value before any boost.

    Best straight bets is the other ranking — the edge, where the chance beats what the price needs — and only rows with a positive edge qualify. Both fours are graded separately in the record. Click any pick card or table row to load that matchup into the form.

    The four at the top are the four to look at, not the four to bet. Two markets on the same game are one bet in disguise — a run line and an under both cash on a pitchers' duel — so a row that shares a game with a higher one is flagged corr. Re-read the inputs on anything that ranks first: in this project a spectacular number has more often been a typo than an edge.
