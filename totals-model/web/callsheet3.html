The moneyline is the split. The book's total says how many runs; the book's moneyline says whose. Together they pin down each side's expected runs, and every other market here is arithmetic on that pair. Without a moneyline there is no run line and no moneyline call.

This matchup the pick, then the rest by edge

The pick is the likeliest side among the markets the sheet's own inputs inform, and only where those inputs move its chance off the book's number by a point or more toward that side. A side the sheet has no information on (the moneyline and the run or puck line in baseball and hockey) is never the pick, however likely it looks: that would be the book's number read back. The label names the kind of game it is (over, under, first five or first period, spread, moneyline, upset) and the why gives the book's number, the sheet's, the lean, and the reasoning. The other markets fold under it, ranked by edge: each row shows how likely that side is to hit and, in gold or red, how far above or below the price's break-even that is. A 62% favourite at −185 is a 62% chance and a losing bet; a 53% under at −110 is the better bet on the same game. The green mark is this game's parlay leg — Call Sheet #1's BET on the total when that side clears its price, otherwise the side with the best chance against its price. Two grey chips on the total are labels, not adjustments: cold-under profile (total 7 or lower, wind in at 10 mph or more, both pens under 3.75, a last ten below the line) and same lean or split lean (whether the full-game pick and the first-five pick agree). An amber over against the grain chip marks an over that the book's prices, the last ten, or 80%+ of the money leans against — overs like that are 11-15 on the season book against 79-44 for the rest. None of these moves a number; each has its own line in the record below, so it can earn one. The three labels are baseball's; the hockey book starts with none and earns its own from the record.

    NBA player props

    Run NBA props.bat writes nba-props-DATE.json: every player prop the books post on the day's games (points, rebounds, assists, threes, points + rebounds + assists), the carded book's line and prices, and beside each the slate's own projection. Rate × minutes: his per-minute rate this season with last season's as a prior at half its minutes, times the minutes of his last five games. Regulars out, scored: every 30 minutes a game of teammates missing who play 24 or more adds 1.42 minutes (t 36), 0.56 points per 36 (t 11) and 0.09 assists (t 6); rebounds and threes did not move, so they get no lift. Pace, the matchup's against the league's, in full. Defence v position from the ledger's own table, what each defence allowed per minute to guards, forwards and centres against the league: a defence's lean over the first half of 2025-26 carried into its second half at 0.77 for points, 0.74 rebounds, 0.73 assists and 0.41 threes, and those are the weights; HashtagBasketball's rank is shown beside it, never scored. Home and road is shown and never scored: a player's own split over the first half of 2025-26 predicted the second half's at r 0.08. A projection built this way is unbiased on every market over 2025-26 and misses by k × √projection, one sigma, k measured per market (1.85 points, 1.2 rebounds, 1.15 assists, 1.1 threes, 1.9 combined), a normal that held 69% within one sigma; that chance is blended with the book's own at 4 to 2, the market the spine as everywhere on this page. The next morning's grade file carries every player's box line, so Load slate on it grades these rows with the card. A prop is the slate's call as made: the page does not rescore it, and the record tile below is the test. Props cannot be backtested against lines (ten credits a market a game on the history endpoint), so the tile earns everything from day one.

    The parlay four the likeliest side on each game at any price, alternate totals included — one a game, likeliest first; each card says whether its price agrees and names the value side it passed over
    Best straight bets #1's BET or better first, then the WNBA cheaper side, then the best edges — up to eight
    Likeliest at any price the most likely outcome in each game, whichever market it sits in, with what its price needs — one a game, likeliest first; not the sheet's pick
    Two-leg parlays up to six pairs from the likeliest legs, different games, ranked by the chance both land — the parlay's price and what it needs beside each
    Upset watch every dog on the date with a measured signal against its favourite — the dog's backup in net is scored (hockey, +5 points), the rest are watched; the dog's price and the sheet's chance beside each
    Alternate totals with a verdict hockey: the alternate number on the sheet's side that reads STRONG BET or MAX BET with an edge, one a game — the band is the chance and the price already asks for most of it, so read the edge; graded on its own record line, never the pick or a leg
    One sport only the same two rules, one sport at a time — only on a night with more than one

    The parlay four, from 19 Oct 2026, takes the likeliest side on each game at any price, whichever market it sits in and the alternate totals included — one leg a game, likeliest first, up to four. Each card says whether the price agrees (the chance clears what the price needs) or is the book's price, and names the value side it passed over, which is often the less likely result at the better number. Before 19 Oct the four were the best value legs with #1's verdicts first, and under that rule they went 17-14-2 in MLB and 13-16 in the NHL; the record tile grades the four as the board now builds them. All four hit is the product of the chances, pays is the book's multiplied price and what it needs, and worth N× is the product of the ratios, the parlay's value before any boost: with four favourites it will usually sit under 1, because the book's margin is in every leg. A boosted payout above the fair multiple beats fair outright.

    Best straight bets is the other list, up to eight, in three tiers. First, every full-game total Call Sheet #1 calls BET or better, best edge first, with the green chip on it — including one whose price is steeper than its chance, which stays on the list with a line saying so, because the verdict tier is the sheet's best record and a verdict at −125 has won before (MLB only: a hockey verdict has no record behind it and lists only when it also clears its price). Second, in the WNBA, the book's cheaper side of the spread — a rule, not a probability: on a −105/−115 spread the book has shaded its price toward the side it wants money on, and taking that side went 12-1 on the first thirteen; it carries a chip, its own record line on the spread tile, and a review at 40 picks. Third, every other pickable market with a positive edge, best edge first, marked as here on the price alone. Which markets each sport picks: MLB the total and the first five; WNBA the spread and the moneyline, which carry the four ratings from 2 Oct. The rest are shown, not picked — the MLB moneyline and run line (22-30 on the first 52 against a claimed 52.5%), the WNBA total (6-7 on the first 13, leaning under by construction). They stay on the rail, the table and the record so the tally keeps running, and come back if the record says so. Both lists are graded separately in the record. Click any pick card or table row to load that matchup into the form.

    The four at the top are the four to look at, not the four to bet. Two markets on the same game are one bet in disguise — a run line and an under both cash on a pitchers' duel — so a row that shares a game with a higher one is flagged corr. Re-read the inputs on anything that ranks first: in this project a spectacular number has more often been a typo than an edge.

    Tonight's card

    CLOSE is the number the market closed at, full-game total and first five, typed the next morning with the finals. It is never locked and it moves nothing. The record below then keeps beat the close per market: positive when the close moved away from the side taken — an over at 7 that closed 7.5, an under at 6.5 that closed 6. A pick that beats the close on average is an edge whatever last night did; one that loses to it on average is luck however the week went. Beating the close by half a run on a total is roughly two points of win probability.
