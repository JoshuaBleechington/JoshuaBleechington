The moneyline is the split. The book's total says how many runs; the book's moneyline says whose. Together they pin down each side's expected runs, and every other market here is arithmetic on that pair. Without a moneyline there is no run line and no moneyline call.

This matchup, ranked by edge

Probability is the big number; edge is the order. Each row shows how likely the pick is to hit and, in gold or red, how far above or below the price's break-even that is. A 62% favourite at −185 is a 62% chance and a losing bet; a 53% under at −110 is the better bet on the same game. The green mark is this game's parlay leg — Call Sheet #1's BET on the total when that side clears its price, otherwise the side with the best chance against its price. Two grey chips on the total are labels, not adjustments: cold-under profile (total 7 or lower, wind in at 10 mph or more, both pens under 3.75, a last ten below the line) and same lean or split lean (whether the full-game pick and the first-five pick agree). An amber over against the grain chip marks an over that the book's prices, the last ten, or 80%+ of the money leans against — overs like that are 11-15 on the season book against 79-44 for the rest. None of these moves a number; each has its own line in the record below, so it can earn one. The three labels are baseball's; the hockey book starts with none and earns its own from the record.

Why

    Day board

    The parlay four #1's BET on the total first, else the best value side — one per game, at any price, only if it clears that price
    Best straight bets #1's BET or better first, then the WNBA cheaper side, then the best edges — up to eight
    One sport only the same two rules, one sport at a time — only on a night with more than one

    The parlay four takes one leg per game, in two tiers. First, Call Sheet #1's verdict: a full-game total #1 calls BET, STRONG BET or MAX BET, on #1's side, when that side also clears its price — the one mark on the board that carries #1's corroboration gate. Second, on a game with no such total: the side, of the markets that sport picks, with the best chance against what its price needs — never a market that is shown, not picked. There is no price cap: a −200 side that clears −200 is a leg like any other, and a +130 side that does not clear +130 is not. In both tiers only a side that clears its price qualifies — a parlay's return is the product of those ratios, a boost multiplies the whole thing, and a leg priced above its chance drags it down however often it hits, so a 55% over at −120 beats a 61% dog at −155. Verdict legs rank first, then by value; the card names what each leg passed on. On a thin night you get three, or two, and the card says why. All four hit is the product of the chances — a boosted payout above that multiple beats fair outright — and worth N× is the product of the ratios, the parlay's value before any boost.

    Best straight bets is the other list, up to eight, in three tiers. First, every full-game total Call Sheet #1 calls BET or better, best edge first, with the green chip on it — including one whose price is steeper than its chance, which stays on the list with a line saying so, because the verdict tier is the sheet's best record and a verdict at −125 has won before (MLB only: a hockey verdict has no record behind it and lists only when it also clears its price). Second, in the WNBA, the book's cheaper side of the spread — a rule, not a probability: on a −105/−115 spread the book has shaded its price toward the side it wants money on, and taking that side went 12-1 on the first thirteen; it carries a chip, its own record line on the spread tile, and a review at 40 picks. Third, every other pickable market with a positive edge, best edge first, marked as here on the price alone. Which markets each sport picks: MLB the total and the first five; WNBA the spread and the moneyline, which carry the four ratings from 2 Oct. The rest are shown, not picked — the MLB moneyline and run line (22-30 on the first 52 against a claimed 52.5%), the WNBA total (6-7 on the first 13, leaning under by construction). They stay on the rail, the table and the record so the tally keeps running, and come back if the record says so. Both lists are graded separately in the record. Click any pick card or table row to load that matchup into the form.

    The four at the top are the four to look at, not the four to bet. Two markets on the same game are one bet in disguise — a run line and an under both cash on a pitchers' duel — so a row that shares a game with a higher one is flagged corr. Re-read the inputs on anything that ranks first: in this project a spectacular number has more often been a typo than an edge.

    Tonight's card

    CLOSE is the number the market closed at, full-game total and first five, typed the next morning with the finals. It is never locked and it moves nothing. The record below then keeps beat the close per market: positive when the close moved away from the side taken — an over at 7 that closed 7.5, an under at 6.5 that closed 6. A pick that beats the close on average is an edge whatever last night did; one that loses to it on average is luck however the week went. Beating the close by half a run on a total is roughly two points of win probability.
