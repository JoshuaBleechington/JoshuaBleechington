Middles the over at one number and the under at a higher one · the apps' numbers now, the legs you hold

No odds on the row — load the odds file (Run NBA odds) and the apps' numbers come with it.
about this section

A middle is the over at one number and the under at a higher one: the final between them wins both legs, outside them one leg loses and the other wins, so the pair pays when the final lands between often enough to cover the juice (4.8% of the time at -110 on both legs). Two seasons of lines history say the four apps sit within a point of each other at tip in 99% of games, and a one-point gap loses 3% of the stake; the money is the move, a number that has run three points or more from a leg you already hold (8-9% both win at 3.5 to 4.5 points, +3% of the stake; 17-20% at five or more, +12-14%). The sheet's normal at 18 points sized those chances within a point of what landed, so every pair here is priced off it, pushes on an integer line included. The first leg is a bet you wanted anyway, placed early; the watch names the second once the number has run, and the second leg gives up its vig to take the loss off the table. The apps' numbers follow the newest odds run on the row; the logged line stays as the markets were scored.

The crowd on Kalshi the exchange's price beside the book's and Pinnacle's · the sharp side on watch · shown, not scored

No Kalshi price on the row — load the slate or the odds file and Fill form, and the exchange's game-winner market comes with it.
about this section

A Kalshi contract pays a dollar if the club wins and trades in cents, so its price is the crowd's chance with no vig and the bid-ask spread is the only juice; the fee is taken on the win, 7% of the expected profit a contract, so a yes at 57 cents needs 58.7% to break even. The card reads each side three ways, the crowd, the book's own devigged moneyline and Pinnacle's, says which price is better after the fee, and when the crowd sits three points or more from Pinnacle names the sharp side as a watch with its own line on the moneyline tile. The ladders (the spread, the total and, off the Game Props tab, each club's team total) are the crowd's whole distribution: the number at even money is its spread, total or team total, the chance at the book's number is read straight between two rungs, and on a rung each side's price is set against the book's after the fee. Shown, not scored: a prediction market's price is a market price, and the market is already the sheet's spine.

Each line is a club's rank against the other 29 on a number the slate already holds: offensive and defensive rating, pace, points scored and allowed, the last ten, free-throw rate, three-point rate, home and road records, the regulars out. A club shows up when it sits in the top or bottom six of the league; the stage of the season and the two watched clubs always. The tag is the point. "3rd worst defence" is what the public bets and the book moves to, so each line says what the sheet already did with the fact: faded because the market over-moves with early-season ratings, scored when a stage delta is on the line, shown because it measured inside the number against two seasons of closes, or a label. Nothing here moves a number on its own.

Filled by the slate ratings, pace, games played, rest — open to check or edit

The four ratings are each club's points scored and allowed per 100 possessions this season, from the league ledger, with the pace beside them; the projection is each offence into the defence it faces over the two paces' mean. Faded, tagged: against two seasons of closes the market over-moved with early-season ratings (the final ran 0.22 a point the other way per point of lean under fifty games a side, t −2.6, both seasons; nothing after fifty), so the lean, shrunk by games played, goes on the line at −0.25 a point until fifty games and is shown after. The four travel together, ten games a side. Rest is a label: back to backs, the second night on the road, three in four and time zones all flipped sign between seasons. The stage of the season moves the total on its own from the date on the row: +1.0 after the trade deadline, +2.0 in the last two weeks, both tagged (933 and 224 games, both seasons the same way). Two clubs, the Nuggets and the Jazz, ran over the close both seasons home and away: a label on the row, not a number, since a club's own over/under record does not carry within a season. The spread and the moneyline are the book's own numbers through a normal margin at 12.5 points: measured priced against everything in the box score, so they are shown and graded, never the pick. The total and the first half are the pickable markets.
