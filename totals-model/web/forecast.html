Call Sheet

Blends the posted number with everything else you can find, then names a side and says how sure it is. Every card gets an answer.

The game posted F5 total

Outlier · games Covers odds Vegas Insider

The first-five number, not the full game. Books post it separately and it usually sits near 4.5. If you only have the full-game total, multiply by 0.54 — F5 carries about 54% of a game’s runs.

Starting pitchers the biggest input

Outlier · matchup panel RotoWire · lineups TeamRankings · runs/game

This is what first-five is for. Take the bullpen out of a total and the two starters are most of what is left — and they are the most predictable thing in the sport, known hours ahead. Season ERA is fine here; the model converts it to runs over five innings and calibrates so two league-average arms project the league-average F5 total.

Runs/game is optional but worth the ten seconds. ERA alone cannot tell a 5.10 offence from a 3.60 one, and each starter is scored against the lineup he actually faces.

Form & head to head enter full-game

Outlier · L5 / H2H tabs Covers · matchups

Enter these as full-game combined totals — that is what every site publishes — and the model converts them to five innings. Asking you for a number nobody prints is how inputs end up guessed at.

Both measured null against the residual on 116 logged games, form at t = −0.07 and head to head at t = −0.40. They are weighted to move a close card, not to overturn the market and the starters together. Leave them blank and their weight redistributes automatically.

Park & weather tonight only

RotoWire · weather Statcast · park factors Ballpark Pal

Read the hourly row, never the card headline. “8 mph wind blowing in West Sacramento” is a card whose own hourly row says out — that “in” is the preposition. Nothing counts under 8 mph; across counts as zero.

Park factor scales the statistical estimates and the wind, and never the posted line — the line already holds the park, and counting it twice is the exact double count that put the old fourteen-input model behind the naked number.

Last 10 the load-bearing input

Covers · WNBA matchups TeamRankings · points/game Basketball Reference

Each side’s combined game total over its last ten — both teams’ points added, not just theirs. Ten games is a third of a WNBA season, which is why this carries the largest non-market weight here: there is no starting-pitcher equivalent to lean on.

Head to head optional

Leave it blank if they have not met. The model does not stall and does not guess — the head-to-head weight is simply redistributed across the market and the last ten, which then carry the whole blend between them. You still get a side and a percentage; it is just built from fewer things.

Absences worth more here than anywhere

ESPN · WNBA injuries RotoWire · injury report

2.0 points off the total per rotation player, 3.5 if it is their leading scorer. A twelve-deep roster with starters at 32+ minutes has no bench to absorb a loss, so the same news costs a WNBA team more than it costs any other league’s team.

Check whether the line already moved on it. When Caitlin Clark was ruled out the Fever line went −11 to −6 on the announcement. If the number you typed in was posted after the news, it is already in there.
