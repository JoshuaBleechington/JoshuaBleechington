Call Sheet

Anchors on what the market is really on — the prices, not the rounded number — then adjusts for what today changes. MLB attacks the total; NFL attacks the spread, because NFL margins are lumpy enough that pricing the key numbers beats guessing the winner. Names a side on every card and grades itself once the games land.

The number prices matter more than the line

Outlier · games Covers odds Vegas Insider

Enter both prices. They are the most valuable thing on the board and the old model threw them away. A posted total is rounded to the half run; the prices are not. Over −120 with the under at +100 says fair sits well above the number that got posted, and the model solves for exactly how far.

Opened at is shown, never scored. The current line is the anchor, so a move is already inside it — subtracting it again would double-count. It is here because a big move against your side is worth seeing before you bet.

Starting pitchers scored as a differential

Innings behind each ERA. Fill them in and a short season gets pulled toward league average — a 5.24 in 22 innings is a reading with a ±1.7 error bar, not a fact. Leave them blank and the ERA goes in at face value, exactly as before.

Both arms or neither. These are scored as a gap from a league-average starter, not as a projection of runs. Two average starters move the line by exactly zero — that is arithmetic, not a calibration that came out right, and it is the fix for the permanent over-lean the previous model carried.

Runs/game is optional and worth the ten seconds: ERA alone cannot tell a 5.10 offence from a 3.60 one, and each arm is scored against the lineup it faces.

Same differential treatment, over the roughly 3.6 innings a pen covers. First-five existed partly to delete this half of the game; a full-game market cannot, and the bullpen is where a lot of nine-inning totals are actually decided.

Combined game totals — both teams' runs added. Both measured null against the residual on 116 logged games, form at t = −0.07 and head to head at t = −0.40, so they are small and say so. One meeting still counts: h2h weight scales by meetings ÷ 4, so enter it and let the model discount it.

Park & weather the one unpriced input

RotoWire · weather Statcast · park factors Action · public betting

Wind is the only thing on this page the market prices imperfectly, because it changes after the number posts. Everything else here is published and already inside the line. Read the hourly row, never the card headline — “8 mph wind blowing in West Sacramento” is a card whose own hourly row says out.

Park factor scales the differentials and the wind, never the market anchor. The posted number already holds the park; counting it twice is the double count that put the old fourteen-input model behind the naked line.

The number home team's spread

Enter the spread from the HOME team's side. −3.5 means the home side is favoured by three and a half; +6 means they are getting six. The model works in margin — home points minus away points — throughout.

This model attacks the spread, not the total, and the reason is the shape of the sport. NFL margins are violently lumpy: a margin of exactly 3 happens in about 9.5% of games and 7 in about 6.5%. Totals have nothing like it. A model that prices that lumpiness can tell you what half a point is worth without having to out-guess the market about who wins.

Power rating net points per game

PFR · team stats TeamRankings · net points

Points scored minus points allowed, per game, for the season. Both teams or neither — it is scored as a differential, so two league-average sides move the number by exactly zero.

Games played is not optional, it is the whole honesty of this input. A net-points rating earns full weight at eight games and a fraction of it before that. In week 2 it is one game of noise and the model will say so by barely moving.

Quarterback shown, never scored

ESPN · injuries RotoWire · injuries

A starting quarterback is the largest single swing in the sport, worth roughly 5 points — and it is deliberately not scored here. An NFL line moves three to seven points on the announcement, so it is already inside the number you are reading and counting it again would double it.

Use it as a check instead. Tick the box, then look at whether the line actually moved. If a starter is out and the number has not moved, the market does not believe the report — and you should find out why before it does.

Saving draft saved

Nothing is lost when you close the tab. What you are typing is written to this browser after every keystroke and comes back when you reopen. The card is stored the same way. Nothing to press.

Rescore re-runs every game on the card through the model as it stands today, from the inputs each row already stores. A row keeps the band it was given when you added it, so after a change to the model a card can hold calls the current model would not make — rows like that are marked. Finals and results are never touched.

Browser storage is not a vault. It does not follow you to your phone and it goes away if you clear site data. Take a backup file at the end of a night you care about — that is the copy that survives a new laptop.

    Alternate lines priced off the main number

    This is the one thing here that does not need the model to be right. A book prices its main line sharply — that is the single fact this project has actually established. It prices the alternate ladder off a template, and templates are coarse. Given the market's own fair number, recovered from the two main-line prices, the fair price at every other rung is arithmetic on the same distribution.

    Enter the MAIN line and its two prices above, then the alt you are being offered. Positive cents means the book is giving you a better price than its own main number implies. That is a relative judgement — it needs the main line to be efficient and nothing else.
